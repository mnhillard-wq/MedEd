Practice histories for presenting to Dr. Cole

No recent patient to present? Use one of these. Each is a set of interview notes in the order the patient told the story. Your job is to turn the notes into an organized oral presentation and to finish with an assessment of your own. All three patients are fictional.

How to use these
  1. Pick one patient and read the notes.
  2. Reorder them into a presentation: opening statement, source, HPI, past medical and surgical history, medications, allergies, social and family history, review of systems, exam, assessment.
  3. Build your assessment: a one-sentence problem representation, then a prioritized differential with your reasons. You do not need to be sure of the diagnosis.
  4. Present to Dr. Cole, then say "done."

Practice history 1Outpatient · urgent visit

Corinne Ashgrove

34-year-old woman, "burning when I pee"

Source: the patient, who gave a clear history.

  1. Burning with urination for 2 days, 6 out of 10. Going every hour, small amounts, with a strong urge each time.
  2. Frequency woke her twice last night and made her miss a work meeting this morning, so she called for a visit.
  3. Bought phenazopyridine (AZO) at the drugstore yesterday. The burning eased a little and her urine turned orange.
  4. No fever, chills, back or side pain, nausea, or vomiting. Has not seen blood in her urine.
  5. No vaginal discharge, itching, or odor. One male partner, her husband. Levonorgestrel IUD placed after her son was born. Last period 2 weeks ago, regular.
  6. Had one urinary tract infection 3 years ago, treated with antibiotics, resolved.
  7. Migraines since her twenties; takes sumatriptan 50 mg about twice a month. Takes a daily vitamin D supplement.
  8. Cesarean delivery 4 years ago. No other surgeries or hospitalizations.
  9. Allergic to sulfa drugs: hives.
  10. Graphic designer. Lives with her husband and 4-year-old son. Never smoked, 1 to 2 glasses of wine a week, no drug use.
  11. Mother has high blood pressure; father has type 2 diabetes.
  12. No headache, rash, or joint pain this week. Otherwise feels well.

T 37.0 °C · HR 78 · BP 118/72 · RR 14 · SpO₂ 99% · Comfortable. Mild suprapubic tenderness, no costovertebral angle tenderness. Urine dipstick in clinic: positive leukocyte esterase and nitrites, trace blood. Urine pregnancy test negative.

Your turn: give a one-sentence problem representation (who, the key features, and how she is doing now), then a prioritized differential: what you think is most likely and why, then one or two others and why they are lower on your list.

Practice history 2Outpatient · same-day visit

Walter Castellano

61-year-old man, "my big toe is killing me"

Source: the patient and his wife, who came with him.

  1. Woke 2 nights ago with severe pain in the right big toe. It is red, hot, and swollen. Even the bedsheet touching it hurts. Pain 9 out of 10.
  2. The night before, he was at a family party: several beers and a steak dinner.
  3. Took ibuprofen 400 mg three times yesterday, which helped a little.
  4. Came in today because he could not get his shoe on for work.
  5. Had two milder episodes of pain in the same toe in the past year, each gone on its own within a few days. Never saw anyone for them. No injury, no cuts or scrapes on the foot. No other joints hurt. No fever or chills.
  6. High blood pressure for 10 years and high cholesterol.
  7. Takes hydrochlorothiazide 25 mg daily and atorvastatin 20 mg daily. Fish oil capsule daily.
  8. Appendectomy at age 22. No other surgeries.
  9. No known drug allergies.
  10. Hospital pharmacist, lives with his wife. Quit smoking 20 years ago after 15 pack-years. 4 to 5 beers on weekends. No drug use.
  11. Brother has gout. Father died of a heart attack at 70.
  12. No rash, back pain, urinary symptoms, or kidney stones. Rest of the review of systems negative.

T 37.4 °C · HR 88 · BP 146/90 · RR 16 · SpO₂ 98% · BMI 31 · Uncomfortable, walking with a limp. Right first toe joint warm, swollen, shiny, and discolored, very tender to light touch; skin intact; other joints normal.

Your turn: give a one-sentence problem representation (who, the key features, and how he is doing now), then a prioritized differential: what you think is most likely and why, then one or two others and why they are lower on your list.

Lyle Brandvold

27-year-old man, "my back is still sore"

  4. Booked the visit because it is improving more slowly than he hoped and he has a 6-hour drive next week.
  5. Pain does not go down his legs. No numbness, tingling, or weakness. No trouble urinating or controlling his bowels. No numbness in the groin.
  6. No fever, weight loss, or pain that wakes him at night. No history of cancer. No injection drug use.
  7. Mild asthma since childhood; uses an albuterol inhaler about once a month. No vitamins or supplements.
  8. No surgeries or hospitalizations.
  9. Allergic to penicillin: rash as a child.
  10. Software tester, lives with a roommate. Plays recreational soccer but stopped since the injury. Never smoked. 3 to 4 drinks on weekends, occasional cannabis.
  11. Father had surgery for a herniated disc at 50. Mother is healthy.
  12. No abdominal pain, urinary symptoms, or rash. Rest of the review of systems negative.
