Review the note

HPI Outpatient Clinic: New Patient History and Physical
Patient: NOVAK, Kayla · DOB: 10/02/2006 · Age: 19 · Sex: F
Historian: Patient, reliable · Author: PGY-1 (clinic)

Chief concern

Headaches for about 10 weeks.

History of present illness

Kayla is a 19-year-old college freshman with about 10 weeks of headache, beginning gradually around the start of the semester in early September. No prior history of headaches beyond occasional mild ones. Initially about twice a week, most days by mid-October, and daily for the past few weeks. Headache is bilateral, a dull, constant pressure behind the eyes and across the back of the head toward the neck. 4/10 now, 7/10 in the mornings. Worst on waking, eases by lunch, returns in the evening. Has woken her from sleep a couple of times. Worse lying flat (now sleeps on a second pillow), with bending over, coughing, and straining; sitting up helps somewhat. Never sudden or thunderclap in onset. Bright light mildly bothersome; noise is not. Associated with mild nausea some mornings without vomiting, an achy neck with full range of motion, fatigue, and difficulty concentrating (rereading material). Reports a whooshing sound in both ears, left more than right, in time with her heartbeat, for about a month, worse lying flat at night. Reports intermittent horizontal double vision on far left gaze, 5-6 episodes over the past 2 weeks, resolving when she looks straight ahead. Seen at student health about 5 weeks ago by a nurse practitioner: told tension headaches, advised ibuprofen and more sleep. Blood pressure checked; no eye exam, no imaging. Has taken ibuprofen 400 mg 5-6 days/week since, sometimes twice daily; helped for 1-2 weeks, no longer. Acetaminophen a few times without effect. Improved sleep, hydration, and reduced screen time without change. Caffeine 2-3 coffees/day. Decided to return after an event at soccer practice 2 days ago.

Review of systems

Denies fever, chills, vomiting, neck stiffness limiting movement, head injury, seizures, syncope, dizziness or lightheadedness, weakness, numbness or tingling, speech difficulty, gait difficulty, eye pain, red eye, flashes or floaters, hearing loss, rash, recent illness, recent travel, and leg swelling.

Past medical history

Acne since about age 14, followed by dermatology. Seasonal allergies (spring pollen). No surgeries, hospitalizations, or concussions.

Medications

Combined oral contraceptive (name not recalled), started by her pediatrician about 8 months ago. Ibuprofen 400 mg most days (see HPI).

Allergies

NKDA. Seasonal pollen.

Family history

Mother: migraines, about monthly, needs a dark room. Father: hypertension. Maternal aunt: died of a brain tumor at 41 (patient was 12). Brother (14): healthy. Maternal grandmother: type 2 diabetes. No family history of blood clots or clotting disorders.

Social history

First-year student at the state university, undeclared. Lives in a dorm with a roommate; family about 40 minutes away. Works weekends at a coffee shop. Club soccer twice weekly (down from 5 days/week in high school). Diet mostly dining hall. Drives; has a car. Alcohol a couple of drinks a couple of times a month. Never smoked or vaped. Cannabis tried a few times. No other drugs. Sexually active with one male partner for about 7 months; OCP and condoms. LMP about 2 weeks ago, regular on OCP. Sleep: in bed around 11, slow to fall asleep, 6-7 hours, wakes early with headache. Mood: stressed with school, denies depression, denies SI.

Physical exam

Vitals: BP 118/72, HR 78, RR 14, T 37.0 C, SpO2 99% RA. Ht 5'4" (163 cm), Wt 145 lb (65.8 kg), BMI 24.9 General: Well appearing, no acute distress. Neck: Supple, no meningismus. Neuro: Alert and oriented. Strength 5/5 throughout. Sensation intact. Coordination normal. Reflexes 2+ and symmetric. Gait normal.
