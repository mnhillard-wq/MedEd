## Case header
Kayla Novak, 19-year-old woman, outpatient clinic, headache and visual changes. Leading diagnosis: idiopathic intracranial hypertension precipitated by doxycycline, with vision-threatening field loss. Can't-miss: cerebral venous sinus thrombosis (combined oral contraceptive in the note). Other alternatives: mass lesion, medication-overuse headache, tension headache or migraine.

## Central grading question
Did the learner recognize that this is not the student-health tension headache, and synthesize the doxycycline exposure, the transient visual obscurations, the papilledema, the abduction deficit, and the field constriction into raised intracranial pressure with a vision threat, name venous sinus thrombosis as the diagnosis not to miss, say plainly what changed from the checkpoint, and plan venous imaging before lumbar puncture with urgent eye evaluation?

## Data availability statement (scope line)
Evidence is the written note only. The learner has the inherited note, their checkpoint, what they obtained in the Step 3 interview, and the platform exam. There are no labs or imaging. Never shown: the gap register, imaging or lumbar puncture results, and the final diagnosis. A gated finding the learner never elicited is a history-taking issue assessed in Step 3, not a failure of this note.

## Gated findings
Doxycycline, transient visual obscurations, bumping into things, and the eye exam findings are named in the demonstrated sets because learners cannot see the rubric. A learner should not lose points in this note for a finding they never had the chance to obtain.

## Assessment summary guidance (paste into the separate summary field)
Three behaviors carry this note: stating what changed from the checkpoint and tying it to specific new findings, naming venous sinus thrombosis as the diagnosis not to miss with a reason from the record, and naming doxycycline as the likely precipitant. Lead the coaching with these, not with the criterion count. A learner who met most criteria but wrote no real statement of change should be told that is the single next step, since it is the point of the assignment. Weight the coaching toward the Assessment section. A learner who names the correct diagnoses without linking them to findings has shown pattern recognition; say what would have made it reasoning, for example connecting the combined oral contraceptive and the raised-pressure picture to venous thrombosis, or connecting the field constriction to urgency. Separate content from expression: a learner who reasoned well but buried the change in a long paragraph needs different coaching from one who omitted it. History and exam were copied or summarized by design; do not coach copy-forward. If new findings are missing from the History or Physical Exam, say they are not documented and point to the Step 3 interview as where they are earned; do not coach the missed question or maneuver again here. Do not comment on imaging or lumbar puncture results, which the learner never had. The narrative shapes coaching and does not override any criterion judgment.
