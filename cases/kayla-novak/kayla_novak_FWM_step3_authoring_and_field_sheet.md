# Kayla Novak, Find What's Missing: Step 3 Repeat Interview
### Assessment authoring sheet and Assessment Studio field sheet, v0.1 draft, 29 Sep 2026
Criterion text lives in `kayla_novak_FWM_step3_repeat_interview_rubric.txt`. Upload or paste that file as the rubric source. It is not duplicated here.

## Case header
Kayla Novak, 19-year-old woman, outpatient clinic. Presenting complaint: headache and visual changes (her exact words are in the base Knowledge Bank opening story, which I did not have). Leading diagnosis: idiopathic intracranial hypertension precipitated by doxycycline, with vision-threatening field loss. Main alternatives: cerebral venous sinus thrombosis (can't-miss, combined oral contraceptive in the note), mass lesion, medication-overuse headache, tension headache or migraine.

## Central grading question
Did the learner recognize that this is not the student-health tension headache, go past what the inherited note says, and elicit the doxycycline exposure, the transient visual obscurations, and the bumping into things, then examine the optic discs, eye movements, and visual fields?

## Data availability statement (scope line)
The learner has the inherited note (a complete-looking note from a PGY-1 that documents the raised-pressure headache pattern, the whooshing, the left-gaze double vision, and no eye exam), the checkpoint they wrote, Kayla in the encounter, and the platform exam. There are no labs or imaging. Never shown to the learner: the gap register, the diagnosis, and any imaging or lumbar puncture result. This rubric scores information gathering and examination only. Interpretation, diagnosis, and plan are scored in the Step 4 note. A gated finding the learner never elicited is a Step 3 issue and is charged here once.

## Gated findings
FWM1 doxycycline, FWM2 transient visual obscurations, FWM4 bumping into things, FWM5 the eye exam findings. FWM3 (weight gain) was retired on 30 Sep 2026 and the codes are not renumbered. The rubric names them because learners cannot see it. Documented facts the learner should not have to re-earn: morning and positional headache, Valsalva worsening, pulsatile whooshing, left-gaze double vision, ibuprofen use, the birth control, the aunt's brain tumor.

## Weighting
Registry default for History and Physical is tiered roughly 50/30/20 across thirteen domains. This rubric departs deliberately. The five gaps and the eye exam are the whole point of the module, and the documented history is intentionally unscored, so seven criteria carry all 100 points with 76 in tier one. Reason: feedback should concentrate on the novel information.

## Optional add (not built)
An interview-method criterion (opens broadly, follows what the patient raises) could be added in tier one. It would raise the item count to nine and dilute the novelty focus, so it is left out unless you want it.

## Assessment summary guidance (paste into the separate summary field)
Two behaviors carry this case: finding the doxycycline through a medication follow-up, and examining the optic discs. Lead the coaching with these before anything else, even when the learner met most other criteria. A learner who obtained the vision history but missed the doxycycline or skipped fundoscopy should hear that as the single next step, not as one gap among several. Weight the coaching toward the history and eye exam items, since the whole rubric concerns what the note did not say. When a learner accepted a documented negative such as "no dizziness" or stopped at the first medication answer, name that specific move and what asking once more would have opened. Do not coach the diagnosis, the differential, or the plan; those are scored in the Step 4 note. Do not coach re-taking documented history. Do not comment on findings the learner never had the chance to see. If a learner reached the eye exam findings but never elicited the history behind them, say so as a history gap and do not treat the exam as incomplete. If a learner asked for the right maneuver only in general terms ("check her eyes"), coach the specificity, since naming the discs, movements, and fields is what turns a request into a plan. Keep the narrative short. It shapes coaching and does not override any criterion judgment.

### Instructor guidance (paste into the Instructor guidance field)
Purpose: Assess whether the learner, starting from a complete-looking note written by someone who never examined her eyes, found what the note does not say. Score information gathering and examination only. Do not score diagnosis or plan.

Evidence and crediting: Credit an item when the information emerged because of something the learner did, whether a direct question, an open question with a narrowing follow-up, or pursuing something Kayla said. If Kayla volunteers a listed finding, score it Demonstrated unless the learner ignored it. When a learner asks several questions in one turn, credit only what Kayla answered. Credit exam maneuvers the learner performs, orders, or states must be done, under any reasonable name. Credit semantic equivalents and speech recognition artifacts.

What matters most: The medication follow-up that reaches the doxycycline, the vision-specific questioning that reaches the transient visual obscurations and and the bumping into things, and the eye exam (discs, movements, fields).

Context to honor: The learner has read the note and Kayla has already told her story once today. A learner who builds on the note instead of repeating it is doing the right thing. A general vision question and a question confirming the documented double vision are different; only a real vision question earns the obscurations.

Do not overemphasize: Documented history the learner did not re-ask. Interview style. Interpretation of findings, diagnosis, and plan. Exam systems outside the eyes.
