Pediatric Reasoning Cases

Ten-minute dialogic reasoning cases led by an AI teacher

Each session is a short, one-on-one case discussion with Dr. Bell, an AI clinician-teacher. The cases are built on curriculum content from the Council on Medical Student Education in Pediatrics (COMSEP). Dr. Bell presents a patient, asks you to commit to a judgment, explain what drives it, and decide what to do next, and adds brief teaching points along the way. Expect a teaching conversation, not an oral exam.

How a session works

  1. Start the conversation by voice or by typing. Each session runs about 10 minutes.
  2. Choose your case. Tell Dr. Bell the age, setting, or problem you want to practice, for example "a toddler," "the emergency department," or "a child with a limp." Or say "you pick," and Dr. Bell will choose one.
  3. Dr. Bell describes the patient. Ask for the history, exam findings, or tests you need. You get the information that moves the reasoning forward, not a full chart.
  4. Think out loud. Commit to where you are leaning, say what supports it, and name what you would not want to miss. Uncertainty is fine; a lean still counts.
  5. Dr. Bell closes with one or two takeaways you can use on your next patient.

Talk with Dr. Bell

Use headphones in a quiet room. Your browser will ask for microphone access when you start by voice.

What you can ask for

Age
Newborn, infant, toddler, school age, adolescent
Setting
Well visit, sick visit, emergency department, newborn nursery, inpatient floor
Problem
Fever, cough, sore throat, ear pain, abdominal pain, diarrhea and dehydration, rash, limp and joint pain, heart murmur, bleeding, blood or protein in the urine, poor growth, speech delay, behavior and sleep, newborn jaundice and breathing, nutrition and feeding, adolescent health, ingestions, injuries, and child safety

If no case matches your request closely, Dr. Bell will say so and offer the nearest one.

Before you start

  • This is practice for clinical reasoning, not guidance for the care of real patients.
  • Do not share information about real patients. Conversations are recorded and processed by the voice service.
  • Dr. Bell can make mistakes. Check anything that matters against a trusted reference or your attending.
