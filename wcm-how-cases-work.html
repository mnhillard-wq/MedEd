Welcome to Reasoning Lab

Overview

Cases span multiple learner levels, specialties, and clinical skills. This guide walks through how each activity type works, how learners are assessed, and how faculty can adapt cases for their own curriculum.

Platform Orientation

Activity Types

Activity typeWhat it involves
History & PhysicalLearner interviews the patient and performs/interprets a physical exam via chat or voice.
Clinical InterviewHistory-only encounter with no physical exam.
Oral Presentation & DiscussionStructured case presentation to an attending, followed by Q&A.
Clinical Reasoning DiscussionA more Socratic, less formal exchange with an attending — brief summary, differential, and plan, with probing follow-ups.
Orders / ManagementLearner enters diagnostic/treatment orders and reviews results as they evolve over a simulated timeline.
Assessment & Feedback

How Learners Are Evaluated

Every activity type produces feedback in one of two forms — formative feedback tied to exact moments in the encounter, and structured assessment data scored against a rubric. Both draw on the same three feedback domains.

Feedback domainWhat it captures
History-TakingDiagnostic information gathered and case-specific discriminators; missed items are flagged with direct transcript evidence.
CommunicationBehavior-level coaching against a MIRS-based checklist, including suggested rewording of specific phrases.
Clinical ReasoningProblem representation and differential diagnosis, with evidence for and against each item on the list.

Where rubrics come from

Rubrics are built from validated instruments — the MIRS checklist for communication, and instructor-generated history-taking checklists that AI helps draft and refine.

Want to use your own rubric?Instructors can also add institutional rubrics for use with their own learners, helping promote consistency across an internal curriculum or program.
Customization & Control

What Faculty Can Change

Every case is built from a set of components, and faculty control what matters most while AI handles the rest of the drafting.

ComponentWhat it defines
Case DesignWhat are we teaching? Learning objectives, differential, vitals/labs, management plan, attending notes.
Clinical DataWhat facts does the patient know? Chief concern, timeline, symptoms, history, what they will and won’t volunteer.
AI-SP PersonaWho is this person? Opening line, demeanor, emotional phases, what matters to them beyond the medical issue.
Feedback PrioritiesWhich feedback domains matter most for this case — history-taking, communication, or clinical reasoning — and how heavily each is weighted.
Structured AssessmentsWhat rubric-scored data gets captured and reported back for tracking at the learner, case, or program level.
ComplexityHow difficult the encounter is, spanning communication complexity (patient behavior, tone) and clinical/reasoning complexity (diagnostic and management demands).

Built to be adapted

Building a new case is easy with AI assistance that enables instructors to specify priorities and feel confident about all data provided to students. Cases can also be shared for use only, shared with edit access for co-design, or copied and modified independently.
