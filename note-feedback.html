The sample note

History

Chief concern: Headaches for about 10 weeks. HPI: 19-year-old college freshman with 10 weeks of daily headache, worse in the morning and lying flat, worse with coughing and bending. Whooshing in both ears in time with her heartbeat for a month. Horizontal double vision on far left gaze. Ibuprofen 400 mg 5 to 6 days a week with no lasting relief. Medications: Combined oral contraceptive. Ibuprofen. Doxycycline twice daily since July for acne. Added after speaking with Kayla: She has been bumping into things, including a mirror and the hallway.

Physical exam

Vitals: BP 118/72, HR 78, RR 14, T 37.0 C, SpO2 99% RA. BMI 24.9. Well appearing. Neck supple. Neuro normal. Added from my examination: Fundoscopy shows bilateral papilledema. Mild left abduction deficit with diplopia on left gaze. Fields constricted bilaterally, worse on the left.

Assessment

19-year-old woman on doxycycline with weeks of positional daily headache, whooshing, and bilateral papilledema with field constriction. Most likely intracranial hypertension. Cannot miss a mass lesion, given a daily headache that wakes her and her aunt's brain tumor. When I first read the note I favored migraine. The papilledema and the abduction deficit changed my leading diagnosis.

Plan

MRI brain, then lumbar puncture with opening pressure. Hold doxycycline. Refer to ophthalmology.
